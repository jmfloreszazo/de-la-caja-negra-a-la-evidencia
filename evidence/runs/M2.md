# M2 · Local horizontal scale with a real PostgreSQL ledger

**Executed and validated on 30 October 2026.** [Español](M2.es.md)

**The counterintuitive result is useful evidence:** throughput **decreased** as more .NET service processes were added to the **same runner**. This does not refute horizontal scaling in Azure; it exposes extra coordination and contention when neither runner CPU nor database capacity is increased with the process count.

## Measured p99 latency, milliseconds

| Replicas | Uniform REST | Uniform gRPC | Hot account REST | Hot account gRPC |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 358.058 | 246.554 | 244.529 | 248.724 |
| 2 | 352.036 | 310.186 | 364.508 | 331.248 |
| 4 | 573.697 | 1010.5 | 612.033 | 690.342 |
| 8 | 1152.908 | 1158.944 | 1065.188 | 1121.307 |
| 16 | 1940.251 | 2025.398 | 1912.24 | 2106.017 |

**Conditions:** 256 unique operations per case, closed-loop concurrency 16, REST/JSON or gRPC/Protobuf over HTTP/2 without TLS, one shared PostgreSQL 16.13 database, and 1–16 .NET 10 processes on a **single GitHub Linux runner**. The hot scenario concentrates 50% of operations on one account. The uniform scenario covers multiple accounts in the synthetic set, **not** a measured production banking distribution.

**Independent accounting oracle:** every case was validated for 256 committed operations, 512 postings, 256 outbox events and ledger conservation/projection correctness. An intentionally deleted posting was detected and restored.

**Interpretation:** no claim that gRPC always beats REST. Increasing same-host processes competes for runner CPU and one database. Closed-loop offered load may hide congestion. Small sample, JIT/cache effects, test order and hosted-runner noise mean no statistically meaningful scaling law can be inferred. These numbers **are not E3, a cloud-vs-on-prem benchmark or MIPS parity**.
